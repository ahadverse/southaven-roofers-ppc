Metal roofing has moved well beyond barns and farm buildings. Today's residential metal roofs come in sleek standing seam panels and metal shingles that mimic slate or shake, and more Southaven homeowners are choosing them for their long life and storm performance. But metal isn't the right fit for every home or budget.

## Types of residential metal roofing

### Standing seam
Long vertical panels joined at raised seams, with fasteners hidden underneath. Standing seam is the premium choice: very weather-tight, clean-looking and long-lasting, because there are no exposed screws to loosen over time. It also costs the most.

### Exposed-fastener panels (screw-down)
Corrugated or ribbed panels attached with screws through the face of the metal. They're less expensive and common on outbuildings and some homes, but the rubber washers on the screws degrade over time and need maintenance to prevent leaks.

### Metal shingles and tiles
Stamped metal shaped to look like asphalt shingles, slate, shake or tile. They offer metal's durability with a traditional look that may suit neighborhoods with HOA rules.

### Metal roof types at a glance

| | Standing seam | Exposed-fastener panels | Metal shingles |
|---|---|---|---|

## Benefits of metal roofing in Southaven

- **Long lifespan.** Properly installed metal roofs commonly last 40 to 70 years, far longer than asphalt.
- **Heat reflection.** Reflective finishes can reduce how much solar heat reaches your attic, a real plus in Mississippi summers.
- **Wind resistance.** Interlocking panels and concealed fasteners hold up well against high winds when installed to the manufacturer's specifications.
- **Sheds rain fast.** Smooth panels move heavy downpours off the roof quickly.
- **Low maintenance.** No granules to lose, and concealed-fastener systems need little upkeep.

## Things to consider before choosing metal

- **Upfront cost.** Metal costs considerably more than shingles at installation. It can make sense over the long run, especially if you plan to stay in your home.
- **Hail and cosmetic dents.** Metal resists punctures but can dent in large hail. Many insurance policies exclude cosmetic damage on metal roofs, so check yours.
- **Installer experience.** Metal is less forgiving than shingles. Panel layout, trim details, expansion and flashing all take specialized skill. Ask to see completed metal jobs.
- **Repairs.** Repairing a damaged section can be harder than swapping a few shingles, and color-matching weathered panels can be tricky.
- **Neighborhood rules.** Some HOAs restrict roof materials or colors.

## How metal handles Southaven's climate

Metal performs well in north Mississippi, but only when the details match the weather:

- **Summer heat.** Light colors and reflective "cool roof" finishes bounce back more sunlight than dark shingles. Metal also expands and contracts as it heats and cools, so standing seam systems use clips that let panels move. Poorly fastened panels can develop oil-canning (visible waviness) or loosened screws.
- **Humidity and condensation.** Warm, moist air meeting cool metal can condense on the underside of panels. A quality underlayment over solid decking and good attic ventilation keep that moisture away from the wood.
- **Heavy rain.** Metal sheds downpours fast, which also means more water hits your gutters at once. Make sure gutters and downspouts are sized for it.
- **Straight-line winds.** Wind resistance depends on the fastening pattern and clip spacing specified by the panel manufacturer, especially at the edges and corners. Ask for the system's wind rating as installed.
- **Hail.** Thicker panels and textured finishes hide minor dents better, but large hail can still leave marks. See the insurance notes below.
- **Trees.** Limbs that scrape across a metal roof can scratch through the paint, so keep branches trimmed back.

## Metal roofing gauge and finishes

Two specs to ask about on any metal roofing estimate:

- **Gauge (thickness).** Lower numbers are thicker: 24-gauge steel is thicker and more dent-resistant than 26- or 29-gauge.
- **Coating and paint system.** Galvalume-coated steel with a high-quality paint finish (often PVDF / Kynar-type paint) resists fading and corrosion better than basic polyester paints.

## What affects the cost of a metal roof

Metal estimates vary more than shingle estimates because there are more choices. The biggest factors:

- **System type.** Standing seam costs the most; exposed-fastener panels the least.
- **Gauge, coating and paint.** Thicker steel and premium paint systems add cost but improve dent and fade resistance.
- **Roof shape.** Valleys, hips, dormers, skylights and chimneys each need custom-bent trim and careful flashing, which adds labor.
- **Tear-off and decking.** Removing the old roof and repairing the deck adds to the price but gives the new metal a sound base.
- **Underlayment.** Many metal installs call for a high-temperature underlayment, which costs more than standard felt or synthetic.
- **Installer availability.** Fewer crews specialize in standing seam, and experienced installers are worth paying for.

## Questions to ask a metal roofing contractor

- How many metal roofs have you installed, and can I see one nearby?
- What panel profile, gauge and paint system are you quoting, and what's the paint warranty?
- How are panels attached, and how does the system handle expansion?
- What underlayment goes under the metal?
- How will you flash the chimney, vents and any skylights?
- Are panels factory-made or roll-formed on site?
- What workmanship warranty do you provide, in writing?

## Caring for a metal roof

Metal needs less attention than shingles, but not none. Clear leaves and branches out of valleys and gutters, especially after fall. On exposed-fastener roofs, have the screws and washers checked every few years and after major storms, since loose or cracked washers are a common leak source. Touch up deep scratches with manufacturer-matched paint to prevent rust, and avoid walking on the panels, which can dent them.

## Metal roofing and insurance

Metal roofs that meet impact standards may qualify for insurance discounts with some carriers. At the same time, as noted above, **cosmetic damage exclusions** are common for metal roofs. Talk with your agent about both before you decide, especially in a hail-prone area like DeSoto County.

Questions worth asking your agent before you sign a metal roofing contract:

- Does my policy, or would a new policy, exclude cosmetic damage to a metal roof?
- Is there a premium discount for an impact-rated metal roof, and what paperwork proves the rating?
- How would switching from shingles to metal change my premium overall?
- Will you need anything from the contractor after installation, such as the product's impact rating or an invoice?
