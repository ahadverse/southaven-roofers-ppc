## What hail damage looks like up close

When a roofer inspects for hail, they're looking for:

- **Bruises or soft spots** in the shingle that feel spongy when pressed, where the impact fractured the mat underneath
- **Missing granules** in round or irregular spots, exposing black asphalt
- **Cracks or punctures** in the shingle surface
- **Dents in soft metals:** ridge vents, box vents, pipe flashing, gutters and downspouts
- **Damage to other exposed surfaces** like window screens, AC fins, siding, fences and cars, which helps confirm hail size and direction

Roofers often mark hail hits in a test square (typically 10 feet by 10 feet) on each slope and photograph them. That documentation is what insurance adjusters look for.

## Hail damage vs. normal wear

Adjusters are trained to separate storm damage from aging and defects, and a good roofer documents the difference before the adjuster arrives. Here's how the common look-alikes compare:

| What you see | Hail damage | Something else |
|---|---|---|
| Dark spots where granules are gone | Random pattern, often with a soft or fractured spot underneath | Even wear across a whole slope, usually heaviest on sun-facing sides, points to age |
| Small round craters | Impact marks that line up with dented vents and gutters | Blistering: pops from trapped moisture or heat, often in clusters, with no matching metal dents |
| Cracks in the shingle | Cracks radiating from an impact point | Straight thermal splits or cracks along old nail lines |
| Scuffs and scrapes | Rarely | Foot traffic, rubbing tree limbs or dragged equipment |

This is one reason inspection soon after a storm matters. The longer a roof weathers after hail, the harder it becomes to show which marks came from that storm.

## Why hail damage matters even if the roof isn't leaking

Hail damage doesn't always cause a leak right away. Instead, it:

- **Strips the granules** that protect asphalt from UV rays, so the shingles bake and become brittle faster in the Mississippi sun
- **Fractures the shingle mat,** creating weak spots that crack and leak months or years later
- **Breaks the seal** between shingles, making them more likely to blow off in the next wind storm
- **Can void or complicate manufacturer warranties,** which generally don't cover hail

By the time hail damage causes a leak, the storm may be long past your insurance claim deadline. That's why inspection soon after a hail storm matters.

## Hail damage and your insurance claim

Most homeowners policies in Mississippi cover hail damage, subject to your deductible. The general process:

1. **Get a free inspection** to confirm whether there's real, claim-worthy damage.
2. **File the claim** with your insurer and note the date of the storm.
3. **Meet the adjuster.** Having your roofer there helps make sure all damage, including vents, flashing and gutters, is included.
4. **Review the scope of loss** line by line. If something was missed, your contractor can submit photos and request a supplement.
5. **Complete the work** and submit final invoices to collect any recoverable depreciation.

Pay attention to your **deductible**. Some policies have a separate wind/hail deductible that's a percentage of your dwelling coverage. For a $250,000 dwelling limit, a 2% deductible is $5,000. Knowing this number helps you decide whether a claim makes sense.

## Mistakes that weaken a hail claim

- **Waiting for a leak.** Hail damage often takes months to leak, and by then your filing window may be closing.
- **Throwing away the evidence.** Dented gutters, downspouts and vent caps help prove hail size. Don't replace them before the adjuster sees them.
- **Assuming the house is fine because the car is.** A car parked in the garage tells you nothing about the roof.
- **Signing on the spot.** Read any contract or agreement carefully before signing, especially one that's contingent on your claim, and never with someone you haven't vetted.
- **Letting several strangers walk the roof.** Every extra climb adds scuffs that can muddy the picture. Choose one trusted roofer to inspect and document.
- **Losing track of the storm date.** Write down the date and time you heard hail. Your claim will ask for it.

## Hail damage on metal roofs, gutters and vents

Metal roofs often shrug off hail that would ruin shingles, but they can dent. Many insurers now add **cosmetic damage exclusions** for metal roofs, meaning dents that don't affect function may not be covered. Check your policy wording. Dented gutters, downspouts and vents on any home are often included in hail claims and are strong evidence of hail size.

## Upgrading after hail: impact-resistant shingles

If your roof is being replaced after a hail storm, consider upgrading to **Class 4 impact-resistant shingles**, which are tested under the UL 2218 standard by dropping steel balls up to two inches in diameter. They cost more than standard architectural shingles, but they're more likely to survive the next storm, and some insurers offer premium discounts. One caveat from UL Solutions itself: UL 2218 ratings are based on physical breach damage seen from the back side of the shingle, so the test doesn't account for the dents or granule loss a homeowner or adjuster may still see after a storm. Class 4 shingles are tougher, not hail-proof.
