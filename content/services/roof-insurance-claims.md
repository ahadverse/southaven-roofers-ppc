## The roof insurance claim process, step by step

### 1. Get the roof inspected first
Before you call your insurer, have a roofer inspect and photograph the damage. This tells you whether the damage is real, storm-related and likely to exceed your deductible, so you don't file a claim that goes nowhere.

### 2. Report the claim promptly
Call your insurer or file online. Note the date of the storm, describe the damage, and get a claim number. Most policies require prompt notice.

### 3. Protect the home from further damage
If water is getting in, arrange [emergency tarping](/emergency-roof-repair). Policies generally expect you to take reasonable steps to prevent more damage. Keep receipts.

### 4. Meet the adjuster
Your insurer will send an adjuster to inspect. If possible, have your roofer there. They can point out damage to vents, flashing, gutters and each roof slope so it's included in the report.

### 5. Review the scope of loss
The insurer's estimate (called the scope or statement of loss) lists what they'll pay for. Review it line by line with your roofer. Common items that get missed include drip edge, starter strip, ice-and-water membrane, pipe boots, ridge vent, decking and code-required items.

### 6. Request a supplement if needed
If items were missed or new damage is found during tear-off (like rotten decking), your contractor can submit photos and documentation to request additional payment.

### 7. Complete the work and close the claim
After the work is finished, submit the final invoice. With a replacement cost policy, this is usually what releases the depreciation your insurer held back from the first check.

## Build a claim file before you call

Claims move faster and get disputed less when you're organized from day one. Keep a folder, paper or digital, with:

- **Your policy declarations page,** showing your deductibles, including any separate wind/hail deductible, and whether the roof is covered at replacement cost or actual cash value
- **The date and approximate time of the storm,** plus local news or weather alerts that mention it
- **Photos and video** of the roof from the ground, damaged gutters, siding, window screens, fences, the AC unit and any interior stains
- **"Before" photos** of the roof, if you have them, from a sale listing, an old inspection or your own phone
- **Your roofer's inspection report** and photos from the roof itself
- **Receipts** for tarps, emergency repairs, or hotel stays if the home became unlivable
- **A claim log:** every call and email with the insurer, with the date, the person's name and what was said

That log matters more than most people expect. If an adjuster changes or a promise gets forgotten, your notes keep the claim on track.

## Understanding your deductible

Your deductible is the portion of the loss you pay. Many homeowners are surprised to learn their policy has a **separate wind/hail deductible**, and that it may be a **percentage of your dwelling coverage** rather than a flat amount.

| Dwelling coverage | 1% deductible | 2% deductible | 5% deductible |
|---|---|---|---|
| $200,000 | $2,000 | $4,000 | $10,000 |
| $250,000 | $2,500 | $5,000 | $12,500 |
| $300,000 | $3,000 | $6,000 | $15,000 |

Check your declarations page or ask your agent so you know your number before you file.

Compare that number with your roofer's estimate. If the repair would cost about the same as your deductible or less, filing may not make sense: you'd receive little or nothing, and the claim can still appear in your insurance claims history. If the estimate is well above your deductible, a claim is usually worth pursuing. Your agent can tell you how your insurer treats claims history.

## ACV vs. RCV: why it matters

- **Replacement cost value (RCV)** coverage pays to replace the damaged roof with new materials of like kind and quality. The insurer usually pays the actual cash value first, then releases the withheld depreciation when the work is complete.
- **Actual cash value (ACV)** coverage pays what the roof was worth considering its age. On an older roof, that can be a fraction of the replacement cost.

Some policies switch roofs to ACV coverage once they reach a certain age. Read your policy or ask your agent.

## Who's who in a roof claim

It helps to know whose side everyone is on:

| Person | Who they work for | Their role |
|---|---|---|
| Staff or field adjuster | Your insurance company | Inspects the damage and writes the insurer's estimate |
| Independent adjuster | Hired by your insurer, often after big storms | Same job as a staff adjuster, on contract |
| Public adjuster | You, for a fee | Prepares and negotiates your claim; must be licensed by the Mississippi Insurance Department |
| Roofing contractor | You | Inspects, documents damage, prices the work and does the repair or replacement |
| Insurance agent | Your insurer or an independent agency | Sells and services your policy and explains your coverage; usually doesn't decide the claim |

If you consider a public adjuster, get the fee arrangement in writing before you sign, and confirm the license with the Mississippi Insurance Department.

## Red flags in the claims process

Protect your claim and your wallet:

- **Don't sign an "assignment of benefits"** or contract that gives a contractor control of your claim without reading it carefully.
- **Avoid anyone who offers to waive or cover your deductible.** That can amount to insurance fraud.
- **Don't let a contractor "negotiate" your claim** unless they hold the right license. In Mississippi, public adjusters must be licensed by the Mississippi Insurance Department.
- **Be wary of large upfront payments.** Insurance checks often come in stages; your payment schedule should match.

## When the check arrives

Insurance money for a roof rarely arrives as one check made out only to you. Expect some combination of:

- **A first payment** for the actual cash value, minus your deductible.
- **Your mortgage lender on the check** if you have a home loan. The lender may need to endorse it and, for larger claims, may hold the money and release it in stages after inspections.
- **A later depreciation payment** on replacement cost policies, once you send the final invoice.
- **Supplement payments** if additional damage or missed items were approved.

Call your loan servicer as soon as the claim is approved and ask what they require, such as a signed contract, the contractor's W-9 or a final inspection. Then set your contractor's payment schedule to match when the money actually arrives.

## If your claim is denied or underpaid

Don't panic. You have options:

1. **Ask for the decision in writing,** with the specific reason.
2. **Request a re-inspection** and bring your roofer's photos and documentation.
3. **Use the appraisal clause** if your policy has one, for disputes about the amount of loss.
4. **Contact the Mississippi Insurance Department** if you believe the claim wasn't handled properly.
