Some roof problems can wait for a convenient appointment. Others can't. If a storm has torn shingles off your roof, a limb has punched through it, or water is pouring through your ceiling, every hour of rain makes the damage worse. The priority is getting the roof covered.

Call now to reach a local roofing contractor about emergency tarping and repairs for your Southaven home.

## Safety first

Before anything else:

- **Get everyone out of rooms** with sagging or bulging ceilings.
- **Stay away from downed power lines** and assume any line is live.
- **Turn off electricity** to areas where water is near light fixtures, outlets or ceiling fans, if you can do so safely.
- **Never climb on the roof** during or right after a storm. Wet shingles and damaged decking are dangerous, and lightning can strike well ahead of the rain.
- **If a tree is on the house,** leave the area under it and call your insurer and a tree service. Don't try to remove it yourself.

## Is it an emergency? A quick way to decide

Not every storm problem needs a crew tonight, and knowing the difference helps you make the right call:

| What's happening | How urgent | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Water streaming or steadily dripping through a ceiling | Emergency | Contain water, cut power to nearby fixtures, call about tarping now |
| A tree or large limb has broken through the roof | Emergency | Leave that area, call your insurer and a tree service, then a roofer |
| Bare decking or a large patch of missing shingles, more rain forecast | Urgent: within a day | Call about a tarp before the next rain arrives |
| A few shingles in the yard, no water inside | Soon | Schedule an inspection in the next several days |
| An old ceiling stain with no new water | Not an emergency | Book a regular [roof leak repair](/roof-leak-repair) visit |

When in doubt, call and describe what you see. A roofer can tell you over the phone whether it needs a same-day response.

## What to do while you wait for help

1. **Contain water inside.** Use buckets, totes and towels. If a ceiling is bulging with water, carefully poke a small hole at its lowest point to drain it into a bucket.
2. **Move belongings** away from the leak: electronics, furniture, rugs, documents.
3. **Photograph and video everything** before cleanup: the roof from the ground, the ceiling, the attic if safe, and anything that was damaged.
4. **Keep receipts** for any emergency supplies or services.
5. **Notify your insurer** and get a claim number if the damage came from a storm.

## What to tell the roofer when you call

A few details help the contractor decide how fast to come and what to bring:

- **Your address and nearest cross street,** plus anything that makes the house hard to find in the dark
- **What happened and when:** wind, hail, a falling limb, or a leak that suddenly got worse
- **Where water is coming in** and roughly how fast
- **Your roof type and height:** one or two stories, shingle or metal, steep or low-slope
- **Whether a tree is still on the roof or power lines are involved.** Those need a tree service or the utility first
- **Your claim number,** if you already have one
- **Photos you can text,** which let the roofer size the tarp and materials before arriving

## How professional emergency tarping works

Emergency tarping, sometimes called "dry-in," covers damaged areas so rain can't get in until permanent repairs are done. A properly installed tarp:

- Uses a **heavy-duty, UV-resistant tarp** large enough to extend well past the damaged area and over the ridge where possible, so water can't run underneath
- Is **secured with wood battens** screwed or nailed through the tarp, not just weighed down with bricks or sandbags
- Is **positioned to shed water downhill** like shingles do, not trap it
- May include **temporary decking** if there's a hole in the roof

[[image:usace-blue-roof|Emergency tarps are secured with battens so they can survive wind and rain until permanent repairs are made.]]

Tarping is a temporary measure. Sun, wind and battens all take a toll, and fasteners through the tarp add holes that will be repaired with the permanent fix. Plan to schedule permanent repairs as soon as the weather and your insurance claim allow.

## Roof emergencies in Southaven's weather

Severe weather in the Mid-South often comes in rounds. A spring squall line may be followed by another wave of storms a day or two later, so a roof opened up on Monday needs to be covered before Wednesday's rain. When wind knocks down trees across the city, as a storm did on July 13, 2025, many Southaven homes need help at once and response times can stretch.

Summer brings its own problems. Strong sun and heat break down tarps faster, so a tarp that looked fine in May can be brittle and torn by August. If permanent repairs are delayed by an insurance dispute or a busy season, ask your roofer to check the tarp and re-secure or replace it as needed.

## Mistakes to avoid in a roof emergency

- **Weighing a tarp down with bricks or tires.** They slide off in wind and can damage shingles or fall on someone.
- **Smearing roofing cement on wet shingles.** It won't bond, and it makes the permanent repair harder.
- **Skipping the photos.** Once a tarp is on, the damage underneath is hard to show your insurer.
- **Leaving a tarp up for months.** A tarp buys time, not a permanent fix.
- **Signing a full replacement contract with the tarping crew on the spot.** Agreeing to emergency tarping is one decision. Choosing who replaces your roof is a separate one, and it deserves time to check licenses, insurance and references.

## Emergency repairs and your insurance

Most homeowners policies require you to take **reasonable steps to prevent further damage** after a loss. Emergency tarping is exactly that. When the damage is from a covered event like wind or hail, the reasonable cost of emergency protection is often included in the claim. To protect yourself:

- Photograph the damage before the tarp goes on and after
- Keep itemized receipts
- Tell your adjuster about emergency work that was done
- Don't make permanent repairs until the adjuster has inspected, unless your insurer approves

## After the emergency: permanent repair

Once the roof is protected, the next step is a full inspection to determine the extent of the damage. Depending on what the roofer finds, the permanent fix may be:

- **A targeted repair:** replacing damaged decking, underlayment and shingles in one area ([roof repair](/roof-repair))
- **Storm damage restoration** across multiple slopes ([storm damage roof repair](/storm-damage-roof-repair))
- **A full [roof replacement](/roof-replacement)** when damage is widespread or the roof was already near the end of its life

How quickly the permanent work happens depends on the adjuster's schedule, your insurer's approval and how busy local roofers are after a widespread storm. In the meantime, check the tarp from the ground after every rain or wind event and call if a corner lifts or water returns inside. Open the attic hatch and run fans or a dehumidifier to help wet insulation and decking dry out, and keep a running list of anything else you discover, such as stained ceilings in other rooms, so it can be added to your claim.

## Don't wait

Water damage spreads fast: into insulation, drywall, flooring and framing. In Southaven's humid climate, mold can follow within days. Call now to be connected with a local roofer about emergency tarping and repair, and protect your home before the next round of rain.
