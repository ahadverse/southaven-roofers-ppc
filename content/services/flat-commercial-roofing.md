## Common flat roofing systems

### TPO (thermoplastic polyolefin)
A white or light-colored single-ply membrane with heat-welded seams. TPO is popular on commercial buildings because it reflects sunlight, which helps reduce cooling loads in the Mississippi summer, and its welded seams are strong when installed well.

### EPDM (rubber roofing)
A durable synthetic rubber membrane, usually black, installed in large sheets with adhered or taped seams. EPDM has a long track record and handles temperature swings well.

### Modified bitumen
An asphalt-based roll roofing reinforced with polyester or fiberglass, installed in layers and often torch-applied or self-adhered. It's tough and common on smaller commercial roofs and residential low-slope areas.

### PVC
A single-ply membrane similar to TPO, often chosen for restaurants and buildings with grease or chemical exposure on the roof.

### Roof coatings
Silicone, acrylic or other fluid-applied coatings can restore and extend the life of an existing roof in fair condition, often without a full tear-off.

### Flat roof systems compared

| System | Surface | Seams | Typical life* | Often chosen for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TPO | White, reflective | Heat-welded | ~20–30 years | Retail, offices, warehouses |
| EPDM | Usually black rubber | Adhered or taped | ~20–30 years | Larger roofs, proven track record |
| Modified bitumen | Granulated or smooth asphalt | Torched, hot-mopped or self-adhered | ~15–25 years | Small commercial roofs, porches, additions |
| PVC | White, reflective | Heat-welded | Varies by product | Restaurants and roofs exposed to grease or chemicals |
| Coatings | White or light colors | Seamless over the existing roof | Varies by product and thickness | Restoring a sound but aging roof |

*Life depends heavily on installation quality, drainage and maintenance. Ask your contractor what the specific product's warranty covers.

## Common flat roof problems

- **Ponding water** that sits for more than a couple of days after rain
- **Open or failing seams** in membranes
- **Blisters and bubbles** in modified bitumen or built-up roofs
- **Punctures** from foot traffic, dropped tools or HVAC service
- **Flashing failures** at walls, curbs, rooftop units and drains
- **Clogged drains and scuppers** that back water up
- **Wet insulation** under the membrane, which reduces energy efficiency and leads to more leaks

## How Southaven's weather treats flat roofs

Low-slope roofs face the same storms as the houses around them, but they respond differently:

- **Heavy rain.** With more than 50 inches of rain in a typical year, drainage is everything. A drain or scupper that's half-clogged during a spring downpour can leave inches of water sitting on the roof, adding weight and pushing water into any weak seam.
- **Summer heat and UV.** Dark membranes absorb heat, which raises cooling costs and ages the surface faster. Daily heating and cooling also makes membranes and metal flashing expand and contract, stressing seams and edges over time.
- **Hail.** Hail can bruise modified bitumen, crack aging coatings and dent rooftop HVAC units, vents and edge metal. After a hail storm, have the roof checked even if nothing is leaking yet.
- **Straight-line winds.** Membrane roofs usually fail at the edges and corners first, where uplift is strongest. Secure edge metal and properly fastened or adhered perimeters are what keep a roof on in a strong storm.
- **Debris.** Leaves, limbs and trash collect around drains on flat roofs, especially near mature trees, and blocked drains are a leading cause of ponding.

## Repair, restore or replace?

| Situation | Usually the best option |
|---|---|
| Isolated leak, roof in good shape | Targeted repair of seam, flashing or puncture |
| Aging membrane, dry insulation, sound structure | Restoration with a coating or recover |
| Widespread wet insulation or failures | Full replacement (tear-off) |
| Ponding from poor drainage | Add tapered insulation, crickets or drains |

An infrared scan or core samples can show whether insulation under the membrane is wet, which is key to choosing between restoration and replacement.

## What affects the cost of flat and commercial roof work

Every commercial roof is different, which is why reliable pricing requires a site visit. The main factors:

- **Roof size and the system chosen,** from coatings at the low end to fully adhered single-ply membranes or multi-ply systems
- **How much wet insulation** has to be removed and replaced
- **Insulation upgrades,** including tapered insulation to fix ponding and any insulation required by current energy code on a full replacement
- **Rooftop equipment and penetrations:** HVAC units, curbs, pipes, skylights and drains each need detailed flashing
- **Access and logistics:** building height, crane or lift needs, and where materials can be staged
- **Scheduling constraints,** such as after-hours work to keep a business open

## Commercial roof maintenance

A simple maintenance plan protects your building and budget:

- **Inspect twice a year,** typically spring and fall, and after major storms
- **Keep drains, gutters and scuppers clear,** especially after leaf drop and storms
- **Limit foot traffic** and use walk pads around rooftop equipment
- **Fix small issues quickly,** since one open seam can soak a large area of insulation
- **Keep records** of inspections and repairs for warranty and insurance purposes

## Planning roof work on an occupied building

If customers, tenants or employees will be inside while the roof is worked on, a little planning prevents most headaches:

- **Ask for a phased plan** so only as much roof is opened as can be sealed the same day.
- **Discuss odors and noise.** Some adhesives and torch-applied systems produce fumes, so ask how air intakes on rooftop units will be handled.
- **Protect the inside.** Cover inventory, equipment and workstations under the work area, especially during tear-off.
- **Plan the parking lot.** Dumpsters, material deliveries and lifts take space, so mark off areas and keep customer paths clear.
- **Request a certificate of insurance** from the contractor before work starts, and check any required license on the Mississippi State Board of Contractors website.
- **Tell tenants or staff** the schedule ahead of time, along with a contact person for questions.

## Questions to ask a commercial roofing contractor

Before you approve a bid, get clear answers to these:

1. Did you check for wet insulation, and how (infrared scan, core samples or both)?
2. Which membrane or coating are you proposing, at what thickness, and why is it right for this building?
3. Is this a manufacturer-warranted system, and who handles the warranty inspection and paperwork?
4. How will you deal with ponding areas and the drains or scuppers?
5. What's included at rooftop units, curbs and edge metal?
6. How will you phase the work to keep the building dry and open?
7. What maintenance will the warranty require from us after the job is done?

Comparing bids on these points, not just price, shows you which contractor actually looked closely at your roof.

## Low-slope sections on homes

Many Southaven homes have porch roofs, additions, carports or dormers with too little slope for shingles. Shingles on low slopes are a common source of leaks because water backs up under them. A modified bitumen or single-ply membrane is the right material for those areas, and it can be tied cleanly into the surrounding shingle roof.
