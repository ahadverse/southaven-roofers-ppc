## What to do right now if your roof is leaking

1. **Contain the water.** Put a bucket or tote under the drip and lay towels around it.
2. **Protect your belongings.** Move electronics, furniture and rugs out of the way.
3. **Relieve a bulging ceiling.** If water is pooling above drywall, a small hole at the lowest point lets it drain into a bucket instead of bringing down the ceiling.
4. **Turn off power** to any light fixture or ceiling fan near the leak.
5. **Take photos** of the stain, any wet insulation in the attic and anything that was damaged. This helps with insurance.
6. **Stay off the roof.** Wet shingles are slick, and storms bring lightning and gusty wind.
7. **Call for help.** If water is pouring in, ask about [emergency tarping](/emergency-roof-repair).

## Where roof leaks actually come from

Water rarely comes in exactly where you see the stain. It enters at a weak spot and then travels along rafters, decking or the top of drywall. The most common sources on Southaven homes:

- **Pipe boots.** Rubber collars around plumbing vents crack in the sun and let water run straight down the pipe.
- **Flashing** at chimneys, sidewalls, dormers and skylights that has rusted, pulled loose or was never sealed correctly.
- **Valleys,** where two roof slopes meet and channel large volumes of water.
- **Missing or lifted shingles** after high winds.
- **Exhaust vents and ridge vents** that let in wind-driven rain.
- **Nail pops,** where nails back out and push up through the shingle above them.
- **Clogged gutters** that back water up under the roof edge.

## What the timing of your leak tells a roofer

When and where a leak shows up is one of the best clues to its source. Before the roofer arrives, jot down when the drip started, how hard it was raining and which way the wind was blowing.

| When it leaks | Likely culprits |
|---|---|
| Every rain, even light showers | An open hole: missing shingles, a cracked pipe boot or a failed valley |
| Only in heavy, wind-driven rain | Flashing, lifted shingles or vents that let sideways rain in |
| Hours after the rain stops | Water traveling a long way along rafters, or soaked insulation still draining |
| On cool mornings with no rain | Attic condensation from poor ventilation, not a roof leak |
| At the top of a window or exterior wall | Step or kickout flashing, gutter overflow, or sometimes the window or siding itself |
| Around a bathroom fan or vent pipe | The pipe boot, vent flashing, or a fan that exhausts moist air into the attic |

This is why a quick patch on the spot right above the stain so often fails. The table narrows the search, but the roofer still has to confirm the entry point.

## How a roofer finds a leak

Finding a leak is detective work. A good roofer will:

- **Start in the attic** if possible, looking for water trails, stained decking, wet insulation and daylight around nails or penetrations.
- **Map the stain to the roof above it,** then look upslope, since water runs downhill before it drips.
- **Inspect every penetration and flashing detail** in that area, not just the obvious spot.
- **Water test** if needed, running a hose on one section at a time while someone watches inside.
- **Show you photos** of the problem and explain the fix before starting work.

## Common leak repairs

| Problem | Typical fix |
|---|---|
| Cracked pipe boot | Replace the boot and reseal surrounding shingles |
| Loose or rusted step flashing | Remove shingles, install new step flashing and counterflashing |
| Chimney leak | Rebuild flashing; may need a cricket on wide chimneys |
| Missing shingles | Replace shingles and check underlayment beneath |
| Valley leak | Re-line the valley with ice-and-water membrane and re-shingle |
| Rotten decking | Cut out and replace damaged sheathing before re-roofing the area |

## Don't forget the damage inside

Once the roof is fixed, check what the water touched. Wet attic insulation loses much of its value and can hold moisture against the drywall. Stained drywall may need to be dried, sealed with a stain-blocking primer, or replaced. In our humid climate, anything that stays damp for more than a day or two can start to grow mold. If the leak came from storm damage, interior repairs may be part of your insurance claim, so document everything before you clean up.

## Why Southaven roofs leak when they do

Southaven typically gets more than 50 inches of rain a year, and much of it arrives in hard spring and summer downpours. Heavy rain pushes large volumes of water through valleys and gutters at once, which is when weak spots show themselves. A few local patterns are worth knowing:

- **Spring storm lines** bring wind-driven rain that finds lifted shingles and tired flashing that normal showers never touch.
- **Summer heat** dries out and cracks rubber pipe boots and sealants faster than in cooler climates.
- **Long humid stretches** make attic condensation more likely in homes with blocked soffit vents or too little exhaust, which can look exactly like a leak.
- **Fall leaf drop** from mature oaks and other shade trees, common in older neighborhoods like Carriage Hills and Greenbrook, clogs gutters and valleys and backs water under the roof edge.

## Common mistakes homeowners make with leaks

- **Painting over the stain** without fixing the source. The stain comes back, and the wood behind it keeps rotting.
- **Smearing roofing cement** over the spot that looks guilty. Tar rarely fixes a flashing or boot problem and can trap water.
- **Waiting for a dry season.** Every rain adds to the damage, and insurers generally expect prompt action on storm-related leaks.
- **Skipping the attic.** Wet insulation hidden above the ceiling can hold moisture against drywall long after the drip stops.
- **Fixing the inside first.** New drywall and paint should wait until the roof is repaired and the area has dried out.
- **Assuming a newer roof can't leak.** Installation errors around flashing and penetrations are common. If your roof is under a workmanship warranty, call the original installer first.

## When a leak means it's time for a new roof

A single leak on an otherwise healthy roof is a repair. But if your roof is older and you're chasing leaks in different places every year, you may be spending repair money on a roof that's worn out. Read [9 signs your Southaven roof needs replacing](/blog/signs-you-need-a-new-roof) or ask the roofer for an honest opinion on remaining life.

A simple test: add up what you've spent on leak repairs over the last few years and ask the roofer how many more years the roof realistically has. If the repairs keep coming and the roof is near the end of its typical lifespan, putting that money toward a [roof replacement](/roof-replacement) usually protects the house better than another patch.
