## Types of asphalt shingles

### Three-tab shingles
The traditional flat, single-layer shingle with uniform cutouts. They're the least expensive but also the thinnest, with lower wind ratings and shorter lifespans. Many manufacturers have scaled back three-tab lines, and most re-roofs today use architectural shingles instead.

### Architectural (dimensional) shingles
Made from two or more laminated layers, architectural shingles are thicker and heavier, with a textured look that mimics wood shake or slate. They carry higher wind ratings and longer warranties than three-tab, and they're the go-to choice for most Southaven roof replacements.

### Impact-resistant (Class 4) shingles
These use a rubberized (SBS-modified) asphalt or reinforced mat to resist cracking when hit by hail. They're rated Class 4 under the UL 2218 impact test, the highest rating. They cost more, but in a hail-prone region they can mean the difference between a roof that survives a storm and one that needs a claim. Some insurers offer discounts for them.

### Premium and designer shingles
Heavier, oversized shingles designed to look like slate or cedar shake. They make a strong statement on larger homes and carry the longest warranties, at a higher price.

## How long do shingles last in Southaven?

Shingle warranties often say "30 years" or "lifetime," but real-world life depends on installation, ventilation and weather. As a rough guide:

| Shingle type | Typical real-world life |
|---|---|
| Three-tab | ~15–20 years |
| Architectural | ~20–30 years |
| Impact-resistant (Class 4) | ~20–30+ years |
| Premium / designer | ~25–35+ years |

Intense summer sun and attic heat are the biggest enemies of shingles in north Mississippi. A properly ventilated attic is one of the best ways to get full life from your roof. Read [how long does a roof last in Mississippi](/blog/how-long-does-a-roof-last-mississippi) for more.

## Picking shingles for Southaven's weather

Beyond heat, a few local conditions should shape which shingle you choose and how it's installed:

- **Straight-line winds.** Spring and summer storms bring strong gusts. Every shingle carries a wind rating, and many manufacturers offer a higher wind warranty only when the roof is installed with extra nails per shingle and their matching starter strips. Ask which rating applies to the way your roof will actually be installed.
- **Spring hail.** If you've filed a hail claim before, or you plan to stay in the house for many years, Class 4 shingles are worth pricing alongside standard architectural shingles. Ask your agent about any discount before you decide.
- **Heavy rain.** With more than 50 inches of rain in a typical year, the underlayment and valley details matter as much as the shingle. Ask what goes under the shingles, not just on top.
- **Shade and trees.** Homes under mature trees stay damp longer, which encourages algae and moss. Algae-resistant shingles help, and so does keeping limbs trimmed back from the roof.

## Choosing a shingle color

Color is personal, but a few practical points apply in our climate:

- **Lighter colors** (weathered wood, light gray) reflect more heat than black or dark brown.
- **Algae-resistant shingles** help prevent the black streaks common on humid Southern roofs.
- **Brick homes** often pair well with browns, charcoals and blended tones. Bring a brick sample or photo when you look at shingle boards.
- **Check HOA rules** in neighborhoods that regulate roof colors.

Ask your roofer for full-size samples or photos of finished roofs, since small swatches can look very different in full sun.

## What affects the price of a shingle roof

Roofers price shingle roofs by the **square**, which is 100 square feet of roof surface. Your total depends on how many squares you have and what goes into each one:

- **Shingle line.** Three-tab costs least, architectural is the mid-range standard, and Class 4 and designer shingles cost more per square.
- **Roof size and pitch.** A steep roof needs more safety equipment and time, and a cut-up roof with many valleys wastes more material.
- **Tear-off.** Removing one layer is routine; two layers take more labor and disposal.
- **Decking repairs** found once the old shingles are off.
- **Accessories:** synthetic underlayment, ice-and-water membrane, starter strips, hip and ridge caps, drip edge, pipe boots and ridge vents.
- **System warranties.** Enhanced manufacturer warranties often require the full set of that brand's components, which can change the price.

## What makes a shingle roof last

The shingle brand matters less than the installation. Details that make a big difference:

- **Correct nailing:** right number of nails in the right place (the "nailing strip") for wind resistance
- **Starter strips** at eaves and rakes so edge shingles are sealed down
- **Drip edge** to protect the fascia and direct water into the gutters
- **Ice-and-water membrane** in valleys and around penetrations
- **New flashing and pipe boots,** not reused old ones
- **Balanced attic ventilation:** intake at the soffits, exhaust at the ridge

Many manufacturers offer enhanced warranties when a certified installer uses a complete system of their components. Ask about it.

## Simple care that extends shingle life

- **Keep gutters and valleys clear,** especially after fall leaf drop, so water doesn't back up under the shingle edges.
- **Trim branches** that touch or overhang the roof. Rubbing limbs scrape off granules, and falling limbs crack shingles.
- **Keep soffit and ridge vents open.** Don't let insulation or stored items block airflow in the attic.
- **Skip the pressure washer.** It strips granules and shortens the roof's life.
- **Look after every big storm** for shingle pieces in the yard or granules piling up at downspouts, and call for an inspection if you see either.
