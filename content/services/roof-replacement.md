A roof replacement is one of the biggest investments you'll make in your home, and in Southaven it's also one of the most important. Our hot, humid summers, heavy spring storms and the hail that rolls through DeSoto County most years all take a toll on shingles. When a roof reaches the end of its life, patching it just buys time. A full replacement resets the clock for decades.

This page explains what a proper replacement includes, the signs it's time, what affects the price, and how to make sure the job is done right. When you're ready, one call connects you with a local roofing contractor for a free, no-obligation estimate.

## Signs it's time to replace your roof

Not every leak means you need a new roof. These signs usually point toward replacement rather than repair:

- **Age.** Most architectural asphalt shingle roofs last roughly 20 to 30 years, and older three-tab roofs often less. Sun and heat in north Mississippi can shorten that.
- **Widespread granule loss.** If your gutters collect handfuls of dark, sand-like granules, the shingles are losing their UV protection.
- **Curling, cupping or cracking** across large areas of the roof, not just one spot.
- **Repeated leaks** in different places, even after repairs.
- **Sagging** along the ridge or between rafters, which can mean the decking underneath has failed.
- **Daylight or water stains** in the attic, especially around nails or along the roof deck.
- **Significant hail or wind damage** across multiple slopes after a storm.

If you're seeing one or two of these in a small area, a [roof repair](/roof-repair) may be enough. If you're seeing several across the whole roof, it's usually time to talk about replacement.

## What a quality roof replacement includes

A new roof is more than new shingles. Ask any contractor you're considering to explain each of these steps in their estimate:

1. **Protecting your property.** Tarps over landscaping, a dumpster or trailer placed where it won't damage your driveway, and plywood or covers over windows and AC units where needed.
2. **Full tear-off.** Removing old shingles, underlayment and worn flashing down to the roof deck.
3. **Decking inspection and repair.** Soft, rotten or delaminated sheathing should be replaced. Good estimates list a per-sheet price for decking so there are no surprises.
4. **Underlayment and ice-and-water barrier.** Synthetic underlayment across the deck, with self-adhering membrane in valleys and around penetrations where leaks start.
5. **Drip edge and starter strips** along eaves and rakes, which help resist wind uplift at the roof edges.
6. **New flashing** at walls, chimneys and skylights, plus new pipe boots. Reusing old, cracked boots is a common source of future leaks.
7. **Shingle installation** to the manufacturer's specifications. The nail count and placement matter for both wind resistance and your warranty.
8. **Ventilation.** Balanced intake at the soffits and exhaust at the ridge keeps attic heat down, which matters a lot in a Mississippi summer.
9. **Cleanup and a magnet sweep** of the yard and driveway for stray nails.
10. **A final walkthrough** and paperwork covering both the manufacturer's material warranty and the contractor's workmanship warranty.

## Choosing roofing materials for a Southaven home

Most homes in Southaven, from established neighborhoods like Carriage Hills and Greenbrook to newer subdivisions off Goodman Road, are roofed with **architectural asphalt shingles**. They're affordable, look good on brick homes, and are what local crews install every day. But you have options:

| Material | Typical lifespan | Why Southaven homeowners choose it |
|---|---|---|
| Architectural asphalt shingles | ~20–30 years | Best value; wide color choice; easy to repair |
| Impact-resistant (Class 4) shingles | ~20–30+ years | Better hail resistance; some insurers offer a discount |
| Standing seam metal | ~40–70 years | Long life, reflects heat, sheds rain fast |
| Metal shingles | ~40+ years | Metal durability with a traditional look |

Lifespans are typical ranges and depend on installation, ventilation and weather. If hail is your biggest worry, ask about **Class 4 impact-rated shingles** (tested under UL 2218). ## What affects the cost of a new roof

Roof replacement in Southaven typically costs several thousand dollars for an average home. This Old House puts the average roof replacement in Mississippi at about **$7,763**, with most projects falling between roughly **$6,164 and $18,621** (as of June 2026). Your price can land anywhere in that range, or outside it, depending on:

- **Roof size and pitch.** Steeper roofs take longer and require more safety equipment.
- **Complexity.** Valleys, dormers, skylights and multiple roof lines add labor and flashing.
- **Number of layers to remove** and how much decking needs replacing.
- **Material choice.** Impact-resistant shingles and metal cost more upfront.
- **Ventilation upgrades, gutters and other add-ons.**

## Replacing a roof after storm damage

If hail or wind damaged your roof, part or all of a replacement may be covered by your homeowners insurance. The order of operations matters:

1. Have the roof inspected and the damage photographed **before** you file a claim.
2. File the claim with your insurer and get a claim number.
3. Meet the insurance adjuster, ideally with your roofer present, so nothing is missed.
4. Review the insurance estimate (the "scope") line by line with your contractor.
5. Schedule the work, then submit final invoices to collect any recoverable depreciation if your policy pays replacement cost.

## How to get a fair estimate

Good estimates are specific. Before you sign, make sure yours includes:

- The exact shingle brand, product line and color
- Underlayment type and where ice-and-water membrane goes
- A per-sheet price for any decking replacement
- Flashing, pipe boots, drip edge and ventilation details
- Permit, disposal and cleanup
- Payment schedule (be cautious of large upfront deposits)
- Written warranty terms for materials and workmanship

Mississippi requires a state license from the Mississippi State Board of Contractors for residential roofing jobs over $10,000, so ask for the contractor's license information and proof of insurance and verify them yourself.

## What to expect on replacement day

Knowing the rhythm of the job takes a lot of stress out of it. On a typical Southaven home, the day goes something like this:

- **Early start.** Crews often arrive around sunrise, especially in summer, so the heaviest work happens before the afternoon heat. Expect a delivery truck, a dumpster or trailer, and ladders going up quickly.
- **Tear-off in sections.** A careful crew removes only as much roof as it can cover with underlayment the same day. That matters here, where a sunny morning can turn into an afternoon thunderstorm.
- **Noise and vibration.** Tear-off is loud. Pictures, mirrors and shelf items on upstairs walls can rattle, and a little dust may fall into the attic, so cover anything stored up there.
- **Decking decisions.** If the crew finds rotten sheathing, the foreman should show you before replacing it and note the sheet count for your invoice.
- **Shingles, flashing and ridge.** Once the roof is dried in, new shingles, flashing, pipe boots and ridge vent go on.
- **Cleanup.** Expect a final pickup of debris and a magnet sweep of the lawn, beds and driveway.

A few simple steps help: give neighbors a heads-up about the noise, keep pets inside and away from the work area, and walk the yard yourself afterward for any nails that were missed.
