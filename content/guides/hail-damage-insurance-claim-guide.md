A hail claim isn't one phone call. It can run from the afternoon of the storm until months later, when the last depreciation check clears, with deductibles, adjusters, a line-item estimate and maybe a dispute along the way.

This guide walks Southaven homeowners through each stage, from documentation to disputes. It's general information, not legal advice. **Your policy is the rulebook for your claim**, so keep it handy, especially the declarations page. If the storm just passed, start with our checklist on [what to do after a hail storm](/blog/what-to-do-after-hail-storm).

## Why timing matters on a hail claim

Three clocks start when hail hits your roof:

- **Your policy's clock.** Policies typically require "prompt" notice of a loss, and some set a specific window for reporting wind or hail damage. Many also limit how long you have to finish repairs, collect withheld depreciation or file suit. Read the conditions section of your policy or ask your agent. The Mississippi Insurance Department (MID) advises reporting a loss "as soon as possible."
- **The evidence clock.** Fresh hits are easier to see and photograph. Months of weather, and later storms, blur which event caused which mark, and your insurer will ask for a specific date of loss.
- **The contractor clock.** After a widespread storm, roofers and adjusters book up fast.

## The hail claim timeline at a glance

Timing varies by insurer and storm, so treat this as a map, not a schedule.

| Stage | What happens | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Storm day | Hail falls; roof damage often isn't visible from the ground | Note the date and time, photograph damage, contain leaks |
| 2. Inspection | A roofer checks every slope and photographs damage | Ask for a written report with photos labeled by slope |
| 3. Report | You notify your insurer and get a claim number | Give the date of loss; start a call log |
| 4. Adjuster visit | The insurer's adjuster inspects the roof and exterior | Attend, ideally with your roofer |
| 5. Scope of loss | The insurer sends an itemized estimate and usually a first payment | Compare it line by line with your roofer's findings |
| 6. Supplements | Missed items or tear-off damage are submitted | Keep copies of everything sent in your name |
| 7. The work | The roof is repaired or replaced | Sign a written contract; keep invoices and photos |
| 8. Depreciation release | On replacement cost policies, withheld depreciation is paid after completion | Send the final invoice before your policy's deadline |

If you have a mortgage, your lender may also need to endorse checks; our [roof insurance claim help](/roof-insurance-claims) page explains what to ask.

## Build your evidence file

Adjusters decide claims on evidence. The more of it that's dated and tied to one storm, the less room "wear and tear" has to become the explanation.

| Evidence | Why it matters | Tip |
|---|---|---|
| Storm date and time | Your claim needs a date of loss | Screenshot weather alerts and neighbors' texts |
| Wide photos of each side of the house | Shows which directions took hits | Shoot from the street and each corner of the lot |
| Dented gutters, downspouts, vents and flashing | Soft-metal dents help show hail size and direction | Put a coin or tape measure in the frame |
| AC condenser fins | Rows of bent fins are a common hail sign | Photograph at an angle with a flashlight |
| Cars, mailboxes, screens, fences | Supports the hail size on your street | Car damage is a separate auto claim |
| "Before" photos and receipts | Shows the damage is new; tarping costs are often reimbursable | Check old listing photos; keep every receipt |

For what hail does to the shingles themselves, see our [hail damage roof repair](/hail-damage-roof-repair) page.

### How to look up NOAA storm reports

The SPC posts preliminary hail, wind and tornado reports for every day, back to June 1999. Open spc.noaa.gov/climo/reports, enter your date as YYMMDD (April 29, 2025 is **250429**), and look in the hail table for **DeSoto, MS** rows.

Times are in UTC, and each report day starts at 1200 UTC, so a storm before about 6 or 7 a.m. local time appears on the previous date's page. **A missing report doesn't mean hail missed your house**: hail falls in narrow bands and many storms go unreported.

## Deductibles, ACV and RCV: running the numbers

[[image:insurance-policy-review|Your declarations page lists your deductibles and how your roof is valued. Read it before you file.]]

### Percentage wind/hail deductibles

According to the Insurance Information Institute (Triple-I), wind/hail deductibles are most commonly a percentage, typically **1% to 5%**, calculated from the **home's insured value**, not the size of the loss. For a Southaven home with $240,000 in dwelling coverage and a 2% wind/hail deductible:

- $240,000 × 2% = **$4,800 deductible**
- A $3,900 repair pays nothing, because it's under the deductible.
- A $16,800 replacement has the $4,800 subtracted from it.

### ACV vs. RCV, and recoverable depreciation

MID defines **replacement cost** as the cost to repair or rebuild with similar materials without subtracting depreciation, and **actual cash value** as that cost minus depreciation for age and wear. On a replacement cost policy, depreciation is usually "recoverable": held back from the first check and paid once the work is done. On actual cash value, it's gone:

| | Replacement cost (RCV) | Actual cash value (ACV) |
|---|---|---|
| Insurer's replacement estimate | $16,800 | $16,800 |
| Depreciation | $5,200 (held back) | $5,200 (not paid) |
| Deductible (2% of $240,000) | $4,800 | $4,800 |
| First payment | $6,800 | $6,800 |
| Depreciation paid after the work | $5,200 | $0 |
| **Total from insurer** | **$12,000** | **$6,800** |
| **Your out-of-pocket** | **$4,800** | **$10,000** |

These are illustrative numbers, not a quote; for real Mississippi prices, see the [roof replacement cost guide](/roof-replacement-cost-southaven). To collect recoverable depreciation, you usually finish the work and send the final invoice within your policy's time limit. The consumer guide on MID's website notes that with replacement cost coverage, you must replace the property to be reimbursed.

### Roof-age endorsements and cosmetic exclusions

Triple-I notes that for hail, some insurers cover roofs on a depreciated (actual cash value) basis rather than paying for a new roof. This often appears as an endorsement that switches the roof to ACV after a certain age, or a schedule that pays less as the roof ages. A **cosmetic damage exclusion** typically excludes dents that change how a roof looks without affecting how it keeps out water, and is often written for metal roofs. Look for endorsements mentioning "roof surfacing," "payment schedule" or "cosmetic," and ask your agent about any you find.

## Meeting the adjuster

- **Ask who they are.** Get the adjuster's name, company and phone number. MID says that if a non-company adjuster is assigned, make sure they're properly licensed and carry identification. MID's licensing search lets you check.
- **Have your roofer there.** The MID consumer guide says it might help to have your contractor meet with you and the adjuster, and Mississippi law lets a roofing contractor confer with the insurer's representative about the damage.
- **Take notes** on which slopes were walked, whether test squares were marked, and next steps.
- **Don't feel rushed.** The same guide says not to feel pushed to agree with anything you aren't comfortable with.

## Reading the scope of loss

The scope of loss is the insurer's itemized estimate. Each line lists a description, quantity and unit, such as **SQ** (a roofing square, 100 square feet), **LF** (linear feet) or **EA** (each), then the price, depreciation and actual cash value. Check the total squares, the waste factor and whether every slope is included, then go line by line with your roofer. Commonly missed items:

| Line item | What it is | Why it gets missed |
|---|---|---|
| Drip edge | Metal edge flashing at eaves and rakes | Hard to see from the ground |
| Starter strip | First course along eaves and rakes | Assumed to come from field shingles |
| Ice and water barrier | Self-adhering membrane in valleys and at penetrations | Hidden under shingles |
| Pipe boots | Seals around plumbing vents | Listed as "reuse" |
| Vents and ridge vent | Box vents, turbines, ridge vent | Not every vent gets counted |
| Decking | Plywood or OSB under the shingles | Shows only during tear-off |
| Code items | Work the adopted code requires that the old roof lacked | Coverage depends on your policy |

On code items: the City of Southaven Building Department says the city has adopted the **2018 edition of the International Code Council family of codes**, and Mississippi law requires residential roofing contractors to follow applicable building codes. The MID consumer guide warns that policies usually limit coverage for meeting updated codes, so ask your agent whether you have ordinance or law coverage.

### Overhead and profit

**Overhead and profit (O&P)** is a general contractor's markup for coordinating a job: scheduling, supervision, insurance and a margin. Insurers differ on when they include it; it often comes up when roofing is combined with other trades like gutters or siding. If it's missing on a multi-trade job, ask the adjuster in writing why.

### Supplements

A **supplement** asks the insurer to add a missed item, a short quantity or damage found during tear-off, such as broken decking. They succeed on proof: photos taken before material is removed, plus measurements. Your contractor usually submits them, but it's your claim, so get copies of everything sent in your name.

## If your claim is denied or underpaid

1. **Get the decision in writing**, with the reason and the policy language relied on. The date you receive it matters for your contract rights.
2. **Compare the reason with your evidence.** Line a "wear and tear" finding up against your dated photos, soft-metal dents and SPC report.
3. **Request a re-inspection** with new evidence and your roofer present, or get a second roofer's opinion.
4. **Check for an appraisal clause.** The MID consumer guide says to look for one if you and the insurer disagree on the claim's value. In a typical clause, each side picks an appraiser, the two choose an umpire, and agreement by any two sets the amount. Appraisal usually doesn't decide coverage questions; read your policy's wording.
5. **Consider a licensed public adjuster**, who works for you, for a fee, to prepare and negotiate the claim. MID licenses public adjusters, so verify the license and get the fee agreement in writing.
6. **File a complaint with MID.** Its Consumer Services Division takes complaints about claim delays and denials online or by mail, fax or email; call 800-562-2957 to request a form. Have your policy number, claim number and date of loss ready. MID asks for 20 working days to contact the company, and says it **cannot order a company to pay a claim**.

For questions about deadlines or whether a denial was proper, talk to a Mississippi attorney.

## Mississippi's roofing contract protections (HB 1408)

House Bill 1408, approved by the Governor on May 8, 2024 and effective July 1, 2024, amended the Insurance Benefits Roofing Repair Consumer Protection Act. The act applies to residential roofing contractors working on homes for one to four families when insurance is expected to pay all or part of the cost. As amended, it says:

| The rule | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| The contract must include a notice that you may cancel within three business days after receiving your insurer's written notice that all or part of the claim isn't covered | Keep the insurer's letter |
| After cancellation, payments are returned within 10 business days, except for emergency services and insurer-approved repairs already done | Ask up front what counts as emergency work |
| No payment can be required until the three-day period expires | Money demanded first is a warning sign |
| Work beyond the covered damage must be identified in writing before signing, as your responsibility | Upgrades shouldn't surprise you |
| Until you file, a contractor can't represent or negotiate your claim (licensed public adjusters excepted) | You can still include your roofer in talks with your insurer |
| No promising, paying or rebating your deductible as an inducement | "We'll handle your deductible" ends the conversation |
| A post-loss assignment can only make the contractor a co-payee, with itemized work and a capitalized warning | You're giving up certain policy rights, so read it slowly |

Violations are an unfair and deceptive practice under the Mississippi Consumer Protection Law, and consumers can sue. This is a summary, not the statute; read the bill text or ask an attorney.

## Fraud red flags that can sink a claim

If a contractor inflates or invents damage, it's your name on the claim. Walk away from anyone who:

- **Offers to waive or "work around" your deductible**, including by padding the estimate.
- **Suggests adding damage** or blaming old wear on the latest storm. Exaggerating a loss is insurance fraud.
- **Wants your insurance check signed over.** The FTC says never sign your insurance check over to a contractor, and don't rely on one to tell you what's covered.
- **Wants a big deposit or cash.** The Mississippi Attorney General's storm guide says not to pay in full up front and that a required deposit shouldn't exceed 10% or 25% of the job.
- **Claims to represent your insurer or a government agency** without showing identification.
- **Can't show a license.** Residential roofing jobs over $10,000 need a state license; check it with the State Board of Contractors. See [how to choose a roofer in Southaven](/how-to-choose-a-roofer-southaven).

Report suspected insurance fraud to MID (800-562-2957) or the Attorney General's Insurance Integrity Enforcement Bureau (888-528-5780). Contractor scams can go to the Attorney General's Consumer Protection Division (1-800-281-4418).
