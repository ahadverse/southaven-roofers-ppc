Most Southaven roofs are replaced the same way: old shingles off, new underlayment and architectural shingles on, done in a day or two. A **FORTIFIED roof** follows a stricter recipe. It changes how the deck is nailed and sealed, how the edges are locked down and which shingles qualify, and an independent evaluator documents the work so the home can earn an official designation.

That designation is what unlocks **insurance discounts** and the state's new **Strengthen Mississippi Homes** grant. This guide covers what FORTIFIED requires, how it differs from a standard [roof replacement](/roof-replacement), what it adds to the cost, where the grant stands as of October 2026 (short version: most Southaven homeowners can't apply yet), and whether it makes sense in DeSoto County.

## What FORTIFIED is and who runs it

FORTIFIED is a voluntary construction and re-roofing program created by the **Insurance Institute for Business & Home Safety (IBHS)**, a nonprofit research organization supported by property insurers and reinsurers. Its standard is built on storm research and goes beyond most building codes.

There are three levels, and each includes everything in the level before it:

| Level | What it adds | Where it fits best |
|---|---|---|
| FORTIFIED Roof | Stronger deck attachment, a sealed deck, locked-down edges, rated roof cover and vents | Existing homes, during a re-roof |
| FORTIFIED Silver | Anchored chimneys, porches and carports, braced gable ends and pressure-rated garage doors, plus opening protection in hurricane-prone areas | Owners going beyond the roof |
| FORTIFIED Gold | An engineered continuous load path from roof to walls to foundation | Easiest to build into new construction |

IBHS requires a FORTIFIED Roof for every level, noting that as much as 90% of catastrophe-related insurance claims include roof damage. The standard also has separate **High Wind** and **Hurricane** versions, so a FORTIFIED professional should confirm which applies to your address. Projects permitted on or after November 1, 2025 must meet the 2025 FORTIFIED Home Standard.

## What a FORTIFIED Roof requires

FORTIFIED Roof targets three weak points: the deck-to-house connection, the roof edges, and the gaps between deck panels. For a typical shingle roof, IBHS's 2025 Re-Roofing Checklist calls for:

- **A full tear-off.** All existing roofing comes off and damaged wood is replaced. The deck must be at least 7/16 inch thick over framing spaced no more than 24 inches apart; thinner decking is typically re-decked.
- **Ring-shank deck nailing.** The deck is re-nailed with 8d ring-shank nails every 4 inches. IBHS testing shows that switching from smooth nails to ring-shank nails with tighter spacing can double the uplift a roof can withstand.
- **A sealed roof deck,** using tape over every seam with underlayment on top, a two-layer felt system, or peel-and-stick membrane over the whole deck. IBHS says sealing the seams can cut water intrusion by up to 95% if the roof cover blows off.
- **Locked-down edges.** Drip edge goes over the underlayment at eaves and rakes, extends at least 2 inches onto the roof and is nailed every 4 inches, staggered. Starter strips are adhered, not just nailed.
- **High-wind shingles** rated ASTM D3161 Class F or ASTM D7158 Class H, installed with six nails. Metal, tile and low-slope roofs must be rated for site-specific design pressures.
- **Rated vents and full flashing.** Roof vents must pass a wind-driven rain test, with flashing at penetrations, walls, valleys, gables and eaves.
- **Signed compliance forms** from the roofer after installation.

IBHS also requires every FORTIFIED Roof to be installed by a **certified FORTIFIED roofing contractor**, one who has completed FORTIFIED training.

### The optional Hail Supplement

A standard FORTIFIED Roof is a wind and water upgrade; it doesn't require impact-resistant shingles. For the optional **Hail Supplement**, asphalt shingles must be rated Good or Excellent in IBHS's Hail Impact-Resistant Shingle Ratings, which IBHS says outperform typical Class 4 shingles in tests with realistic hailstones. Metal panels need a UL 2218 Class 4 rating.

### How certification works

1. **Choose a certified roofer and an evaluator.** Evaluators are independent third-party inspectors. Some contractors have evaluators they prefer, but IBHS says you should always be free to choose your own.
2. **The evaluator documents the job** as it happens, photographing key steps and product labels.
3. **IBHS reviews the file** and, if the work meets the standard, sends a designation certificate to the evaluator, who delivers it to you.
4. **Renew every five years.** If nothing has changed and the roof cover is within its life expectancy, renewal is typically just a re-designation inspection.

Without that verification, IBHS says, a home can't receive a designation. So FORTIFIED is a decision you make **before** your next re-roof starts.

## FORTIFIED Roof vs. a standard re-roof

Careful roofers already do some of these things. FORTIFIED makes all of them mandatory and has a third party verify them.

| | Typical re-roof | FORTIFIED Roof |
|---|---|---|
| Old roofing | Usually torn off; some codes allow a second layer | All existing roofing removed |
| Deck attachment | Existing nailing often left as-is | Re-nailed with 8d ring-shank nails every 4 inches |
| Deck seams | Covered by underlayment, usually not sealed | Sealed with tape, two-layer felt or full membrane |
| Edges | Drip edge and starter details vary | Drip edge nailed every 4 inches; starter strips adhered |
| Shingle wind rating | Whatever the product carries | Class F or Class H, six nails |
| Roof vents | Standard vents | Tested for wind-driven rain |
| Hail resistance | Optional upgrade | Optional Hail Supplement |
| Verification | Final walkthrough with your roofer | Independent evaluator plus IBHS review |
| What you get | Manufacturer and workmanship warranties | Warranties plus a five-year designation |

## What a FORTIFIED roof costs

No published figure can price your roof, but IBHS offers a rule of thumb: when re-roofing a **2,000-square-foot home, the FORTIFIED upgrade typically adds $1,000 to $3,000**, depending on the home's design and local code.

For context, This Old House's June 2026 Mississippi data puts the **average roof replacement at about $7,763**, with a typical range of **$6,164 to $18,621**, and about **$8,842** for a two-story, 2,000-square-foot home. These come from different sources, so treat them as a rough frame, not a quote.

Plan for these too:

- **The evaluator's fee.** Evaluators set their own prices, so get the fee in writing up front.
- **Decking surprises.** Thin or rotten decking must be replaced, and nobody knows how much until tear-off.
- **Hail Supplement shingles** cost more than standard architectural shingles.

Ask each roofer to price the FORTIFIED items as separate line items. Our [Southaven roof replacement cost guide](/roof-replacement-cost-southaven) explains how to compare bids. If insurance is paying for a storm-damaged roof, the insurer generally covers the damage and you pay for upgrades beyond it, unless your policy has a FORTIFIED endorsement.

## Insurance discounts for FORTIFIED homes in Mississippi

This is where FORTIFIED can pay you back. According to the Mississippi Insurance Department (MID), **state law requires insurance companies to give discounts to homeowners who mitigate their homes to IBHS standards**, and insurers have filed discounts with MID ranging from **15% to 30%** for fortified homes. IBHS says some Mississippi insurers offer as much as 55% off the wind portion of a premium.

Before you count on savings:

- **Discounts typically apply to the wind portion** of your premium, not the whole bill.
- **You need the certificate,** not just FORTIFIED-style work.
- **Keep it current.** IBHS says many insurers require renewal every five years to keep the discount.
- **Amounts vary by company,** and MID's grant instructions encourage homeowners to shop around.

Some policies also include a **FORTIFIED endorsement** that helps pay to upgrade a damaged roof after a covered claim. IBHS lists Mississippi among the states where these are known to be offered.

Before you sign a roofing contract, ask your agent what a FORTIFIED Roof designation would save you per year, whether your policy has a FORTIFIED endorsement, and what documents they need. For more on coverage and claims, see [roof insurance claim help](/roof-insurance-claims).

## The Strengthen Mississippi Homes grant: where it stands in October 2026

**Strengthen Mississippi Homes (SMH)** is a grant program administered by MID under Senate Bill 2409, which took effect July 1, 2026. It helps homeowners upgrade to the FORTIFIED Roof standard and, per MID, is funded by fees from insurance companies, not taxpayer money.

### How the grant works

- **Up to $10,000** toward the roof upgrade; anything above that is yours to pay.
- **Selection by lottery.** Rounds open periodically, with dates announced in advance.
- **Paid to the contractor, not you,** after the work is finished and the paperwork, including the FORTIFIED certificate from IBHS, is submitted.
- **The evaluator's fee is not covered.**
- **Three bids, then an award letter.** You get bids from three SMH-approved contractors and must have the award letter before work begins. Once approved, the roof must be installed within three months to the FORTIFIED Roof High Wind standard.
- **One grant per household,** and it doesn't have to be repaid.

### Who qualifies

- Owner-occupied, single-family homes that are the owner's primary residence in Mississippi.
- Not condominiums, townhomes, duplexes, rentals, or mobile or manufactured homes.
- The home must be in good repair (recent storm damage is an exception), with an in-force homeowners policy that includes wind coverage, plus flood insurance in a special flood hazard area.

### Why most Southaven homeowners can't apply yet

The holdup is funding. MID's June 10, 2026 announcement explained that House Bill 1911, MID's fiscal year 2027 funding bill, allowed up to $15 million in statewide grant money, but because of a typo, MID has no spending authority over those funds until the Legislature reconvenes and fixes it, expected in early 2027.

For now, the program runs on **$2 million from the Mississippi Windstorm Underwriting Association** (MWUA, the state "wind pool"), and applications are open **only to MWUA policyholders** insured by the wind pool for three consecutive policy years. MWUA is one of the largest insurers of coastal homes, and MID describes this phase as fortifying homes on the Gulf Coast. A typical Southaven homeowner with a standard policy isn't eligible. MID says other homeowners will qualify in a **statewide rollout anticipated in early 2027**.

In the meantime:

- **Watch mid.ms.gov.** Click "Mitigation Program Information" on the homepage to reach the SMH page, or sign up for MID email updates.
- **Don't start work hoping for reimbursement.** The grant only pays for work done through the program after an award letter.
- **Don't let a failing roof wait on a lottery.** If your roof is leaking or storm-damaged, fix it now. You can still earn a FORTIFIED designation without the grant by hiring a certified roofer and evaluator yourself.
- **Be skeptical of grant promises.** Recipients are chosen by lottery, and contractors come from the program's approved list.

## How to find a FORTIFIED-trained roofer and evaluator

IBHS's **"Find a Professional" directory** at fortifiedproviders.com, linked from fortifiedhome.org, lets you search by ZIP code. IBHS says listed contractors and evaluators have completed FORTIFIED training and meet minimum requirements for experience, insurance and licensing where required.

As you narrow the list:

- **Confirm certification.** Ask whether the roofer is currently FORTIFIED-certified, then check the directory.
- **For the grant, use MID's lists** of approved evaluators and contractors.
- **Check the state license.** Mississippi requires a Mississippi State Board of Contractors license for residential roofing jobs over $10,000; verify it at msboc.us. Our guide on [how to choose a roofer in Southaven](/how-to-choose-a-roofer-southaven) covers contracts and red flags.
- **Get the FORTIFIED scope in writing:** tear-off, deck nailing, seam sealing, drip edge, starter, shingle product and wind rating, vents, and who pays the evaluator.

When you call, you'll be connected with a local roofing contractor who can inspect your roof and give you a free estimate. Not every roofer is FORTIFIED-certified, so ask on the call, and if a designation matters to you, cross-check the IBHS directory.

## Does FORTIFIED make sense in Southaven's hail and wind climate?

Southaven sits at the top of the state, about as far from the Gulf as Mississippi gets, so hurricanes aren't the main threat. Spring thunderstorms are, with hail, straight-line winds and tornado watches. NOAA storm reports show it: golf-ball-size hail near State Line Road in Southaven on January 25, 2021, and on July 13, 2025, multiple trees down across the city and a semi trailer overturned in a parking lot next to Stateline Road.

How FORTIFIED lines up with those risks:

- **Wind: a strong match.** Peeled-back edges and blown-off shingles are classic straight-line wind damage, and adhered starters, locked-down drip edge, six-nail shingles and ring-shank deck nailing target exactly that.
- **Water: a strong match.** If wind strips shingles, a sealed deck helps keep a downpour out of the attic, which matters in a city that typically sees more than 50 inches of rain a year.
- **Hail: only with the supplement.** For hail, pair FORTIFIED with impact-resistant shingles.

[[image:storm-clouds-suburb|Southaven's biggest roof threats come from spring thunderstorms: hail, straight-line winds and wind-driven rain.]]

### Class 4 shingles as a complementary upgrade

Class 4 is the highest of the four UL 2218 impact ratings, which come from dropping steel balls (up to 2 inches in diameter) onto the shingle. Class 4 shingles resist hail; FORTIFIED keeps the roof attached and the water out. For the FORTIFIED Hail Supplement specifically, choose a shingle rated Good or Excellent by IBHS, not just any Class 4 product.

One limit: UL Solutions notes that UL 2218 ratings are based on breach damage seen from the back of the shingle, so they don't account for dents or granule loss. Impact-resistant shingles are tougher, not hail-proof. See [hail damage roof repair](/hail-damage-roof-repair) for what hail does to shingles, and note that [metal roofing](/metal-roofing) can also be part of a FORTIFIED Roof.

### When FORTIFIED makes the most sense

The upgrade usually offers the best value when your roof is **due for replacement anyway**, you **plan to stay** long enough for insurance savings to add up, your insurer **confirms a meaningful discount**, or you're **replacing after a storm** and want the next one to do less damage. It makes less sense if your roof is only a few years old or you're selling soon.

IBHS is candid that FORTIFIED won't prevent all damage. It reduces the odds that a few lost shingles turn into a soaked attic.
