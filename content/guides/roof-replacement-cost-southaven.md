Ask three Southaven homeowners what they paid for a new roof and you may hear three very different numbers. That's usually not because anyone got ripped off. Roof prices swing with the size and shape of the roof, the material, what's hiding under the old shingles, and whether insurance is paying part of the bill.

This guide pulls together the most current published cost data for Mississippi, explains what pushes a Southaven quote up or down, and shows you how to compare estimates. One honest caveat first: **no website, calculator or phone quote can tell you what your roof will cost.** Only a contractor who has inspected and measured your roof can give you a real number, in writing. If you want to know what the job itself involves, see our [roof replacement](/roof-replacement) page.

## What a new roof costs in Southaven and across Mississippi

This Old House's Mississippi cost guide, updated in June 2026, puts the **average roof replacement in Mississippi at about $7,763**, with prices typically ranging from **$6,164 to $18,621**. The low end of that range matches the state's average asphalt shingle job, and the high end matches the average slate roof.

That matters in Southaven, where most homes, from the brick ranches in Central Gardens to the two-story houses in Brentwood Farms and Lakes of Nicholas, wear architectural asphalt shingles. If you're replacing shingles with shingles, you're shopping in the lower and middle part of the statewide range, not the top.

The same guide estimates cost by home size:

|---|---|---|
| 1,000 sq ft | 1 | $7,158 |
| 1,500 sq ft | 1 | $8,672 |
| 2,000 sq ft | 2 | $8,842 |

These estimates are modeled on a 6/12 roof pitch with one-foot eave overhangs, using national construction cost data. Real Southaven roofs have hips, valleys, dormers and steeper pitches that change the math, so treat the table as a budgeting starting point, not a quote.

Two more numbers from This Old House's 2026 homeowner survey are worth knowing. About **54% of respondents spent between $5,001 and $20,000** on their roof replacement. And while roughly 79% said the final cost matched their estimate, **about 14% said it came in higher than expected.** Hidden damage is usually the reason, which is why the extras section below matters.

## What drives the price of your roof

Roofers price jobs by the **square**, the industry's unit for 100 square feet of roof surface. Your roof's square count is always larger than your home's floor area because of pitch and overhangs. From there, these factors move the number:

| Cost factor | Why it changes the price | What to ask your contractor |
|---|---|---|
| Roof size (squares) | More material, labor and disposal | How many squares did you measure, and what waste factor did you add? |
| Pitch (steepness) | Steep roofs are slower to work and need more safety gear | Is there a steep-slope charge? |
| Complexity | Valleys, hips, dormers and skylights need more cutting and flashing | How will valleys and wall flashing be handled? |
| Stories and access | Two-story walls, tight lots and fences slow tear-off and cleanup | Where will the dumpster or trailer go? |
| Layers to remove | Each old layer adds labor and disposal weight | How many layers did you find, and is removal included? |
| Decking condition | Rotten or delaminated sheathing must be replaced | What is your price per sheet of decking? |
| Material and product line | Upgraded shingles and metal cost more upfront | Which brand and product line is this price based on? |
| Ventilation | New ridge vent or intake vents add material and labor | Does my attic need ventilation changes? |
| Chimneys and walls | Step and counter flashing take time to do right | Will flashing be replaced or reused? |
| Timing | After a big storm, local roofers book up fast | How soon can you start, and is the price locked? |

Two upgrades come up often in Southaven:

- **Impact-resistant (Class 4) shingles** are tested under UL 2218, which drops steel balls up to 2 inches in diameter onto the shingle. ## Hidden and extra costs to plan for

The shingles are the visible part of the bill. The items below are where budgets get surprised. In This Old House's 2026 survey, about 26% of homeowners cited hidden or unexpected roof damage as a major factor in their project cost.

### Decking replacement
Nobody can see the condition of your roof deck until the old shingles come off. Soft, rotten or delaminated plywood or OSB has to be replaced before the new roof goes on, and it's usually billed **per 4x8 sheet**. This Old House estimates that repairing dry-rotted decking typically runs **$500 to $1,000**, depending on the extent of the damage. Get a per-sheet price in writing, and ask the crew to photograph any rotten decking before they replace it.

### Extra layers
If your house already has two layers of shingles, removal takes longer and produces more debris. This Old House notes that tearing off an existing roof takes roughly 1.5 hours per 100 square feet and can add **at least $1,000**, depending on roof size and the contractor's rates. Make sure every estimate says how many layers are coming off and whether removal is included.

### Permits
This Old House cites typical roofing permit costs of **$150 to $500**, but permit rules and fees are set locally. Ask your contractor or check with the City of Southaven Building Department. A reputable roofer pulls any required permit and includes it in the price.

### Disposal and cleanup
Dumpster or dump-trailer fees and landfill charges are normally built into the bid. Confirm that, along with a magnet sweep for nails.

### Ventilation
Southaven summers push attic temperatures high, and poor ventilation shortens shingle life. Many manufacturers' warranties also expect adequate attic ventilation. Adding ridge vent, replacing old box vents or clearing blocked soffit intake adds cost, but it protects your investment.

### Flashing, pipe boots and drip edge
New pipe boots and flashing cost little compared to the leaks old ones cause. Be wary of estimates that say "reuse existing flashing" without explaining why.

### Gutters
Gutters dented by hail or loosened during tear-off may need repair or replacement. That work is often priced separately or handled by a gutter specialist.

## How insurance changes the math after hail or wind

Weather drives a lot of roof replacements here. This Old House estimates that roughly 41% of Mississippi roof replacements are triggered by weather or disaster damage. If a covered storm damaged your roof, your out-of-pocket cost can look very different. Homeowners insurance generally pays for sudden damage from covered perils like hail and wind, not wear and tear. Three things decide what you pay:

- **Your deductible.** Many policies carry a separate wind/hail deductible, and it may be a **percentage of your dwelling coverage** rather than a flat amount. With $250,000 in dwelling coverage and a 2% wind/hail deductible, you'd pay the first $5,000. Check your declarations page before you file.
- **RCV or ACV coverage.** With replacement cost value (RCV) coverage, the insurer usually pays the actual cash value first and releases the withheld depreciation after the work is done. With actual cash value (ACV) coverage, depreciation isn't paid back, so on an older roof the gap between the check and the cost of a new roof can be large. Some policies settle roofs on ACV once they reach a certain age.
- **Upgrades and extras.** If you choose a better shingle, switch to metal, or add work the storm didn't cause, you pay the difference. On insurance-funded roof jobs, Mississippi law requires the roofing contractor to identify in writing, before the contract is signed, any work that goes beyond the covered damage and to explain that those items are your responsibility.

Here's a simplified illustration (not a real claim) of how an RCV claim typically pays out:

| Step | Amount |
|---|---|
| Insurer's replacement cost estimate | $14,000 |
| Minus depreciation held back | $4,000 |
| Minus your deductible | $2,500 |
| **First check (actual cash value payment)** | **$7,500** |
| Depreciation released after the work is finished | $4,000 |
| **Insurer pays in total** | **$11,500** |
| **Your share (the deductible)** | **$2,500** |

One firm rule: **a contractor can't make your deductible disappear.** Mississippi's Insurance Benefits Roofing Repair Consumer Protection Act, as amended in 2024, prohibits a residential roofing contractor from advertising, promising, paying or rebating any part of your deductible to get you to sign. Treat that offer as a reason to walk away.

## Financing a new roof: what to watch for

A roof is rarely a planned expense, so many homeowners finance some or all of it. Financing can be a reasonable tool, but read the terms before you sign anything:

- **Get the cash price first.** Then ask whether the price changes with financing. Some contractor financing programs charge the contractor a fee that can end up built into your price.
- **Compare the total cost, not the monthly payment.** A low payment stretched over many years can cost far more in interest.
- **Understand "no interest if paid in full" offers.** According to the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, if you don't pay off the entire promotional balance in time, interest going back to the date of purchase can be added on top of the remaining balance.
- **Be careful with loans secured by your home.** Home equity loans and lines of credit can carry lower rates, but your house is the collateral.
- **Shop around.** Your own bank or credit union may offer better terms than the option presented at the kitchen table.
- **Don't finance what insurance will cover.** If you have an open claim, find out what the insurer is paying first.

## How to compare 2 or 3 estimates line by line

Mississippi's Attorney General recommends getting at least three estimates, with each contractor bidding on exactly the same work and every bid itemized and detailed. Once you have them, lay them side by side and check that each one spells out:

- **Tear-off** down to the deck, with the number of layers stated
- **Total squares** measured, plus the waste factor
- **Shingle brand, product line and color**, and the impact rating if any
- **Underlayment type**, plus ice-and-water membrane in valleys and around penetrations
- **Starter strip, hip and ridge cap, and drip edge**
- **New flashing** at walls and chimneys, and **new pipe boots**
- **Ventilation plan**: ridge vent, intake and the number of vents
- **Per-sheet decking price**, and how many sheets (if any) are included
- **Permit, disposal, cleanup** and magnet sweep
- **Gutter work**, if needed
- **Manufacturer and workmanship warranties**, in writing
- **Start date, expected duration and payment schedule**
- **License and insurance.** Mississippi requires a state license for residential roofing jobs over $10,000, so get the license number and a certificate of insurance

If one estimate is much lower than the others, find out what's missing. A low bid that skips new flashing, leaves the decking price open-ended or reuses old pipe boots often costs more by the time the job is done. ## Ways to save without cutting corners

- **Replace before it's an emergency.** Nearly 60% of homeowners in This Old House's survey described their roof replacement as urgent or an emergency, which leaves little time to shop around. An inspection while your roof still has a few years left gives you time to compare bids.
- **Avoid the post-storm rush if you can.** After a big spring storm, local roofers are booked with repairs, and it's harder to get multiple estimates quickly.
- **Pick a solid mid-grade architectural shingle.** For most Southaven homes, designer shingles add cost without adding much protection.
- **Ask your agent about discounts** before choosing between standard and impact-resistant shingles. A premium discount can offset part of the upgrade over time.
- **Watch the Strengthen Mississippi Homes program.** This state program offers grants of up to $10,000 toward a FORTIFIED roof. According to a June 2026 Mississippi Insurance Department announcement, the first round is limited to homeowners insured through the state's wind pool, with a statewide rollout anticipated in early 2027. Southaven homeowners most likely can't apply yet, so keep an eye on mid.ms.gov.
- **Fix ventilation during the re-roof.** It's cheaper now than later, and it helps the new roof reach its full life.

What not to skimp on: decking repairs, underlayment, flashing, proper nailing and permits. Skipping any of them saves a little today and costs a lot later. Be cautious of a "layover" (new shingles over old ones), too. It avoids tear-off costs now, but it hides decking problems, adds weight and can complicate warranties, and the next replacement means tearing off two layers.

## When a repair is the cheaper choice

Not every problem calls for a new roof. A repair usually makes financial sense when the roof is relatively young, the damage is limited to one area, and the rest of the shingles still lie flat and hold their granules. A cracked pipe boot, a few wind-lifted shingles or a leak at the chimney flashing are classic repair jobs.

Think about it as cost per year of protection. If a modest repair buys several more good years, it's a bargain. If you're paying for leak after leak on a roof near the end of its life, that money is going into a roof you'll replace anyway. An insurance claim can also change the answer: if a covered storm damaged the whole roof, patching an old roof may not be the better deal.
