Look at the rooftops on almost any Southaven street and you'll see the same pairing: brick walls under architectural asphalt shingles. Lately, though, more homeowners are asking whether a metal roof would be a smarter buy, especially after a spring full of hail warnings or a summer of high cooling bills.

There's no single right answer. Metal and shingles each win in different situations, and the best choice depends on your budget, how long you plan to stay, your HOA and your insurance policy. This guide compares the two honestly for north Mississippi conditions, so you can walk into an estimate knowing what to ask.

## The short answer

- **Choose architectural shingles** if you want the lowest upfront cost, may sell within the next several years, or live where shingles are the norm (or the rule).
- **Choose metal** if you plan to stay 20 years or longer, want the longest-lasting roof you can buy, and are comfortable paying more now to replace less often.
- **Consider Class 4 impact-resistant shingles** if hail is your biggest worry but metal is out of budget.

For a closer look at each material on its own, see our pages on [metal roofing](/metal-roofing) and [asphalt shingle roofing](/shingle-roofing).

## Metal vs. shingles at a glance

| Factor | Architectural asphalt shingles | Metal (standing seam or metal shingles) |
|---|---|---|
| Upfront cost | Lowest; the budget baseline | Higher; Mississippi averages run close to double, and standing seam costs more still |
| Typical lifespan | Roughly 15–30 years, depending on the product, ventilation and storms | Roughly 40–60+ years when installed well |
| Heat and energy | Dark colors soak up heat; lighter or "cool" rated shingles help | Reflective painted finishes shed a lot of solar heat |
| Wind | Good when nailed and sealed to spec; broken seals are the weak point | Very good when fastened and trimmed to the manufacturer's specs |
| Hail | Standard shingles bruise and shed granules; Class 4 lines resist better | Resists punctures but can dent; some policies exclude cosmetic dents |
| Noise | Quiet | Usually only slightly louder over solid decking and an insulated attic |
| Maintenance | Watch for lifted tabs, granule loss and cracked pipe boots | Little upkeep; exposed-fastener panels need screws and washers checked |
| Repairs | Quick and inexpensive; almost any roofer can do them | Harder; matching weathered panels takes a metal specialist |
| Curb appeal and resale on brick homes | The familiar look buyers expect in most Southaven neighborhoods | Dark standing seam can look sharp on brick; metal shingles mimic a traditional look |
| HOA rules | Almost always allowed, though colors may be limited | Check your covenants first; some HOAs restrict metal or certain colors |

These are typical ranges, not promises. Installation quality, attic ventilation and the storms your roof lives through all move the numbers.

## Upfront cost vs. cost per year of roof life

Sticker price is only half the story. A more useful number is **cost per year of roof life**: what you pay divided by how long the roof should last.

This Old House's Mississippi cost data puts asphalt at about **$4.70 per square foot** (an average total of about $6,164) and metal at about **$8.71 per square foot** (about $11,430 on average). The same source estimates asphalt shingles last about 15 to 20 years in Mississippi (up to about 25 for hurricane-rated lines) and metal about 40 to 60 years. Using those figures as an illustration:

| | Average installed cost (Mississippi) | Life used in the math | Rough cost per year |
|---|---|---|---|
| Asphalt shingles | ~$6,164 | 15–25 years | ~$250–$410 |
| Metal | ~$11,430 | 40–60 years | ~$190–$285 |

Two big caveats. First, these are statewide averages, not quotes. Many Southaven homes have larger or more complex roofs, and your real price depends on size, pitch, valleys, decking repairs and the exact product. Our [Southaven roof replacement cost guide](/roof-replacement-cost-southaven) explains what drives the number. Second, cost per year only works in your favor if you're around to collect those extra years. Metal's advantage shows up in year 25, not year 5.

## How each roof handles Mississippi heat

Southaven summers regularly push into the 90s with heavy humidity, and that heat is hard on asphalt. Over time, the sun dries out shingles, the granules wear away, and the roof turns brittle. Good attic ventilation slows that process more than almost anything else, which is why a roofer should check your intake and exhaust vents no matter which material you pick.

Metal with a reflective paint finish handles the sun differently: it bounces back more of the heat instead of absorbing it. ENERGY STAR notes that cool roofs save the most energy in hot, sunny climates like the Southern U.S., and it lists both **shingles and metal** among common cool roof materials. So you don't have to switch to metal to get a cooler roof. If energy use is a priority, ask your contractor about "cool" rated products in either material, and remember that insulation and ventilation affect your bills too.

Humidity matters as well. Shingles in our climate often develop dark algae streaks unless they have algae-resistant granules. With metal, the coating and paint system decide how well the roof resists fading and corrosion, so ask what the finish warranty covers.

## Wind, hail and the dent question

**Wind.** Spring storms in the Mid-South bring straight-line winds that test every roof edge. Shingles hold well when they're nailed in the right spot and their adhesive strips seal, but once a seal breaks, the next gust can crease or tear them off. Concealed-fastener metal panels are very wind-resistant, but only if the edges, ridge and trim are fastened exactly as the manufacturer specifies.

**Hail.** This is where the comparison gets interesting. Hail bruises standard shingles and knocks off their protective granules, which often leads to an insurance claim and, sometimes, a new roof paid for by your policy minus your deductible. Metal rarely gets punctured, but larger hail can leave **dents**, especially in thinner panels.

Most dents are **cosmetic**: they don't leak or shorten the roof's life. That sounds like good news, and mostly it is. The catch is that some insurance policies exclude cosmetic damage to metal roofs, so a hail storm that would have earned a shingle roof a paid replacement might leave a metal roof dented for good, with no payout. Neither outcome is wrong, but you should know which one you're signing up for.

## Impact-resistant shingles: the middle option

If you like the look and price of shingles but worry about hail, **Class 4 impact-resistant shingles** are worth pricing. The rating comes from the **UL 2218** test, which drops steel balls onto the roofing. According to the Insurance Institute for Business & Home Safety (IBHS), the balls range from 1.25 inches for Class 1 up to 2 inches for Class 4, each dropped from a height that matches the energy of a hailstone that size.

Keep expectations realistic. Under UL 2218, a product passes if nothing cracks or tears through to the back of the material. Dents and lost granules on the surface don't count as failures. In IBHS testing of 22 shingle products, impact-rated shingles generally held up better than standard ones, but they weren't damage-proof. Think of Class 4 as **more resistant**, not hail-proof.

Class 4 shingles cost more than standard architectural shingles, but far less than most metal roofs. If you want to go further, our guide to [FORTIFIED roofs in Mississippi](/fortified-roof-mississippi) explains a stronger construction standard that some homeowners pair with impact-rated shingles.

## Which roof fits your plans?

### If you might sell within about 5 to 10 years

Shingles usually make more sense. Most buyers in the Southaven market expect shingles, a newer shingle roof looks good on a home inspection report, and you won't own the house long enough to benefit from metal's extra decades.

### If you plan to stay 20 years or more

This is where metal shines. If you stay 25 or 30 years, a shingle roof installed today would likely need replacing at least once along the way. A well-installed metal roof can carry you through without another tear-off. Our post on [how long a roof lasts in Mississippi](/blog/how-long-does-a-roof-last-mississippi) covers the factors that shorten or extend each material's life.

### If you're somewhere in between

Class 4 shingles are often the sweet spot: a modest premium over standard shingles, better hail performance, and possibly an insurance discount.

## Insurance questions to ask before you choose

Policies vary a lot, so call your agent before you sign a roofing contract and ask:

- **Is there a discount** for Class 4 shingles or an impact-rated metal roof? How much, and what proof do you need?
- **Does my policy exclude cosmetic damage** to metal roofs? If not, could that change at renewal?
- **Is my wind/hail deductible** a flat dollar amount or a percentage of my dwelling coverage?
- **How does roof age affect payment?** Some policies pay older roofs at actual cash value rather than replacement cost.

Ask for the answers in writing or check the policy wording yourself. A roofer can tell you how materials perform, but only your insurer can tell you how they'll be covered.

## Why the installer matters as much as the material

A well-installed shingle roof will outperform a poorly installed metal one every time. Almost every local crew installs shingles daily, so finding a capable shingle installer is relatively easy. Metal is less forgiving. Panel layout, expansion room, flashing at walls and chimneys, and trim details all take specialized experience, and mistakes show up as leaks or wavy panels.

Before you hire anyone for a metal job, ask how many metal roofs they've installed, whether they can show you completed metal projects in DeSoto County, and whether they're approved by the panel manufacturer. For any roof, verify the contractor's license with the Mississippi State Board of Contractors and get the materials and workmanship warranty terms in writing.
